**Problem 1:**

Let $$m$$ and $$n$$ be positive integers.  

For each value of $$m$$ and $$n$$, explicitly describe all possible group homomorphisms from $$\mathbb Z_m$$ to $$\mathbb Z_n$$.  Which of the specific group homomorphisms are ring homomorphisms?

**Problem 2:**

Let $$p(x)\in \mathbb Q[x]$$, and let $$I=(p(x))$$ be the ideal generated by $$p(x)$$.
Prove that if

$$\varphi: \mathbb Q[x]/I\rightarrow\mathbb C$$

is a ring homomorphism, then there exists a complex root $$\lambda$$ of $$p(x)$$ such that

$$\varphi(f(x) + I) = f(\lambda)\quad\text{for all}\ f(x)\in \mathbb Q[x].$$

**Problem 3:**

In this problem, we will prove **Cayley's Theorem** that every finite group turns out to be isomorphic to a subgroup of a symmetric group.

Let $$G$$ be a group with $$n$$ elements and let $$H$$ be the group of **permutations of $$G$$, ie. the set of all bijections from $$G$$ to $$G$$.
Note that a choice of enumeration of the elements of $$G$$ induces an isomorphism of $$H$$ to $$S_n$$.

For each $$g\in G$$, define a function $$L_g$$ by

$$L_g: G\rightarrow G\ \ \ L_g(x) = g*x.$$

a) Prove that for each $$g\in G$$ the function $$L_g$$ is a permutation of $$G$$.  Hence it is an element of $$H$$

b) Prove that if $$g,g'\in G$$ then $$L_{g*g'} = L_g\circ L_{g'}$$

c) Prove that the the function $$\varphi: G\rightarrow H$$ defined by $$\varphi(g) = L_g$$ is a group monomorphism

d) Use the **First Isomorphism Theorem** to explain why (c) implies $$G$$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $$S_n$$


**Problem 4:**

Consider the ring $$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$$.

* (a) Show that $$1+\sqrt{-3}$$ is irreducible.
* (b) Show that $$2$$ is irreducible
* (c) Show that $$1+\sqrt{-3}$$ is not prime.
* (d) Is $$2$$ prime?  Explain.
* (e) Explain why $$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$$ is not a UFD.


**Problem 5:**

Consider the ring of Gaussian integers $$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-1}]$$.

* (a) Find all the units.
* (b) Suppose that $$z,w\in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-1}]$$ are nonzero and satisfy $$\lvert z\rvert > \lvert w\rvert$$ where here $$\lvert\cdot\rvert$$ is the complex absolute value.
Prove that there exist $$x,y\in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-1}]$$ with

$$z = yw + x,\quad\text{and}\quad \lvert x\rvert < \lvert w\rvert.$$

This shows that $$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-1}]$$ has some version of the **division algorithm**, making it what is called a **Euclidean domain**.

* (c) Use (b) to determine a greatest common divisor of $$442+182i$$ and $$-221+91i$$, where $$i=\sqrt{-1}$$.
