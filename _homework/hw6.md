**Problem 1:**

Let $$K$$ be the splitting field of $$x^4-2$$ over $$\mathbb Q$$.

* (a) Prove that there exists an automorphism $$\sigma\in \Gal_{\mathbb Q}(K)$$ with $$\sigma(\sqrt[4]{2}) = \sqrt[4]{2} i$$ and $$\sigma(i)=i$$.
* (b) Let $$\tau$$ be complex conjugation.  Prove that

$$\Gal_{\mathbb Q}(K) = \{\text{id},\sigma,\sigma^2,\sigma^3,\tau,\sigma\tau,\sigma^2\tau,\sigma^3\tau\}.$$

* (c) Prove that $$\text{Gal}_{\mathbb Q}(K)$$ is isomorphic to the dihedral group $$D_4$$.
* (d) Explicitly exhibit the Galois correspondence between subgroups of $$\Gal_{\mathbb Q}(K)$$ and subfields of $$K$$.  Make sure to determine all the subfields and subgroups explicitly and indicate which goes to which.

**Problem 2:**

Let $$p$$ be a prime number and $$n>0$$ and let $$\overline{\mathbb F_p}$$ be an algebraic closure of $$\mathbb F_p$$.
We define $$\mathbb F_{p^n}$$ to be 

$$\mathbb F_{p^n} = \{u\in\overline{\mathbb F_p}: u^{p^n}-u=0\}>$$

* (a) Prove that $$\mathbb F_{p^n}$$ is a field.  (Hint: fixed field of $$\sigma_{Frob}^n$$)
* (b) Prove that the Galois group of $$\mathbb F_{p^n}$$ is a cyclic group generated by $$\sigma_{Frob}$$ and is isomorphic to $$\mathbb Z_n$$.
* (c) Explicitly exhibit the Galois correspondence between subgroups of $$\Gal_{\mathbb F_p}(\mathbb F_{p^n})$$ and subfields of $$\mathbb F_{p^n}$$ when $$n=12$$.  Make sure to determine all the subfields and subgroups explicitly and indicate which goes to which.  

**Problem 3:**

In this problem, we'll prove Cauchy's Theorem 

**Theorem (Cauchy):**  If $$G$$ is a finite group of order $$n$$ and $$p$$ is a prime divisor of $$n$$, then $$G$$ has an element of order $$p$$.

Let $$S$$ be the set of $$p$$-tuples $$(g_1,\dots, g_p)$$ of elements of $$G$$ satisfying $$g_1 * g_2 * \dots * g_p = e$$.

* (a) Prove $$S$$ has $$n^{p-1}$$ elements.
* (b) Define a relation a relation $$\sim$$ on $$S$$ by saying $$(g_1,\dots, g_p)\sim (h_1,\dots, h_p)$$ if and only if one tuple is a cyclic permutation of another.  Prove that this is an equivalence relation on $$S$$.
* (c) Prove that every equivalence class of $$\sim$$ has either $$1$$ element or $$p$$ elements and that the equivalence classes with $$1$$ element must look like $$(a,a,\dots, a)$$ with $$a^p=1$$.
* (d) Count the elements of $$S$$ using equivalence classes to prove that there's at least two equivalence classes with $$1$$ element.  This implies there exists an element of order $$p$$.

**Remark:** It's important that $$p$$ is prime!  For example, $$\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_3$$ has order $$18$$, but no element of order $$9$$.

**Problem 4:**

A group is called **simple** if it has no nontrivial, proper normal subgroups.

In this problem, we will prove that $$A_n$$ is simple for all $$n\geq 5$$.

* (a) If $$N$$ is a normal subgroup of $$A_n$$, explain why if $$N$$ contains a certain cycle type, then $$N$$ contains **all** elements of that cycle type.
* (b) By checking how many elements of cycle type $$2,2$$, $$3$$, and $$5$$ there are in $$S_5$$, use Lagrange's Theorem to prove that $$A_5$$ is simple.
* (c) Prove that for $$n > 5$$, any nontrivial normal subgroup $$N$$ of $$A_n$$ will have a non-identity element $$\sigma\in N$$ satisfying $$\sigma(n)=n$$.
* (d) Use induction to prove that $$A_n$$ is simple for all $$n\geq 5$$.  (Hint: if $$N$$ is normal in $$A_n$$, then consider $$N\cap A_{n-1}$$)
